# Scenario Anti-Patterns

Select 3–5 relevant warnings and make each specific to the brief. Do not paste every category into every document. These guide judgment; the priority rules in `references/refusals.md` govern exceptions.

## Universal

- **Template-first structure:** assigning a hero and feature cards before understanding content. Instead, name the actual page/screen rhythm, list its regions and explain how they support the task.
- **Invented proof:** filling an impressive layout with fictional metrics, customers or testimonials. Use provided proof or an explicitly labelled slot.
- **Evidence laundering:** presenting guessed tokens or catalog claims as observed rendered behavior. Link claims to the exact evidence type and disclose gaps.
- **Ornament without purpose:** adding images, gradients, frames or motion to satisfy a quota. Explain each major visual choice in terms of communication or brand fidelity.
- **Token drift:** one-off values and controls with incompatible behavior. Use semantic roles and documented component states.

## Product / Dense Operations

- Marketing hero, photography and oversized whitespace displacing work controls.
- Status communicated by color alone, clipped table content or focus that disappears in drawers.
- Decorative motion that delays repeated tasks; missing loading, empty, conflict and error states.
- Banning grouping containers instead of evaluating scanability and hierarchy.

## Public Service / Healthcare / Accessibility-Critical

- Novelty prioritized over familiar language and task completion.
- Assuming an official component package automatically proves accessibility.
- Tiny controls, insufficient contrast, pointer-only interactions or color-only validation.
- Treating density as permission to remove zoom/reflow or clear labels.

## Editorial / Heritage / Luxury

- Choosing beige, brass or a display serif merely because of the category; the inverse mistake is deleting them despite explicit brand evidence.
- Type theatrics that harm article reading or CJK glyph coverage.
- Forced image counts and motion competing with the publication's actual content.

## Developer Tools / Documentation

- Decorative terminal chrome or fabricated product screenshots presented as real.
- Assuming every audience wants dark mode or the same sans font.
- Reducing technical readability to generic landing-page spectacle.

## Creative / Consumer / Commerce

- Treating “playful” as a requirement for animation everywhere or “professional” as a ban on friendly typography.
- One-accent rules that erase an established multicolor identity or meaningful category encoding.
- Generated or random photography mistaken for real product evidence.
- Replacing useful repeated product cards with artificial layout variety.

## AI / Finance / Trust-Sensitive Products

- Unsupported speed, accuracy, conversion or security claims.
- Category stereotypes used as evidence for actual brand colors or fonts.
- Trust conveyed only through palette rather than clear content, understandable actions and real sources.
